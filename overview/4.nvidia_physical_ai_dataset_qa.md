# NVIDIA Physical AI Stack for Pure Dataset Creation: Q&A

## Query 1: Stack Requirements for Pure Dataset Activity

**User Question:**
> If one were to purely develop annotated video dataset for Physical AI training, Do we require all of these or a subset. Notice puredatset activity -something like oxford pets data, no model,no training nothing

### Answer

**No, you do not need all of them.** For a pure dataset creation project—similar to Oxford-IIIT Pet but for video and Physical AI—you only need a **subset** of the stack, depending on whether you are creating **synthetic data from scratch** or **annotating and augmenting existing real-world videos**.

---

#### Option 1: Purely Annotating & Augmenting Real-World Videos (No 3D Simulation)
If you already have real-world video footage (e.g., dashcam videos, factory floor cameras, or human hand-object interaction videos) and only want to label, filter, and diversify it into a clean dataset:
* **You only need NVIDIA Cosmos** (specifically **Cosmos Reason** and **Cosmos Curator** / **Cosmos Transfer**).
* **Skip Omniverse and Isaac Sim completely.** Because you are not simulating physics or building 3D environments, you do not need 3D engines or robotics simulators.
* **What Cosmos does here:**
  * **Auto-Annotation & Captioning:** Uses Vision-Language Models (like Cosmos Reason) to automatically generate dense text captions, action labels, and physical reasoning tags for your video clips.
  * **Curation & Deduplication:** Uses Cosmos Curator to split raw footage into clean clips, filter out blurry or useless frames, and remove duplicates.
  * **Visual Augmentation (Optional):** Uses Cosmos Transfer to take your real videos and generate stylistic variations (e.g., turning daytime footage into night or rain) while keeping your ground-truth bounding boxes or segmentation masks intact.

---

#### Option 2: Generating Synthetic Video Datasets from Scratch (3D-to-Video)
If you do not have real-world cameras and want to generate pixel-accurate, automatically labeled video datasets (e.g., exact 2D/3D bounding boxes, depth maps, optical flow, and segmentation masks) in virtual environments:
* **You need Omniverse + Isaac Sim** (specifically the **Omniverse Replicator** API built into Isaac Sim).
* **Skip Cosmos** (unless you later want to make the synthetic renders look more photorealistic).
* **Why you need both Omniverse and Isaac Sim here:** Isaac Sim runs on top of Omniverse libraries. Together, they let you load 3D assets, randomize object positions/textures, run basic physics (like objects falling or colliding), and automatically export video frames paired with 100% accurate ground-truth annotation files (COCO, KITTI, etc.) without manual human labeling.

---

#### Summary Decision Matrix

| Your Starting Point | Tools Required | Tools to Skip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raw real-world videos** that need cleaning, clipping, and auto-labeling | **Cosmos** (Curator + Reason) | Omniverse, Isaac Sim |
| **Raw real-world videos** that also need weather/lighting variations | **Cosmos** (Curator + Reason + Transfer) | Omniverse, Isaac Sim |
| **3D assets/scenes** that need physics and pixel-perfect auto-labels | **Omniverse + Isaac Sim** (Replicator) | Cosmos |

## Query 2: Software Licensing & Library Costs

**User Question:**
> If we keep aside hardware system requirements and cost, does the libs cost anything for create the said datasets?

### Answer

**No, the libraries and models themselves cost $0 to download, run, and use—even for commercial dataset creation.** You do not pay per-frame fees, API token costs, or software licensing fees to generate or annotate datasets.

#### Software & Licensing Breakdown

* **NVIDIA Omniverse:** Free for both individual and commercial production use. As of May 2026, NVIDIA removed the paid enterprise subscription requirement to run Omniverse in production. You only pay if you choose to buy an optional **NVIDIA AI Enterprise** support plan.
* **NVIDIA Isaac Sim (including Replicator):** Completely free and open-source under the **Apache 2.0 license**. There are no user caps or output royalties.
* **NVIDIA Cosmos (Models & Curator/Data Factory tools):** Free to download and run locally. Models are released under permissive open licenses (such as the NVIDIA Open Model License and OpenMDW-1.1), which allow commercial use and state that **NVIDIA claims zero ownership over the datasets or outputs you generate**.

#### Important Caveats & Fine Print

* **Ownership of Generated Datasets:** You retain 100% ownership and commercial rights to the annotated video datasets you produce with these tools.
* **NVIDIA Hardware Requirement:** While the software is free, the Omniverse license terms stipulate that the software is licensed solely to run on systems equipped with **NVIDIA GPUs**.
* **Third-Party 3D Assets:** While the Isaac Sim code is Apache 2.0, some bundled sample 3D assets (environments, props, or textures) carry separate materials licenses. If you distribute raw 3D assets or build datasets for commercial sale, check the asset pack terms or import your own open-license/custom USD assets.
* **Attribution:** If you use Cosmos models to generate or augment your dataset and later distribute the dataset or derivative models, the license requires including a simple `"Built on NVIDIA Cosmos"` attribution notice.
