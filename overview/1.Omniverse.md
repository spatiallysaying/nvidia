Your mental model is 90% spot-on. You have correctly mapped out how a modern digital twin pipeline works in NVIDIA Omniverse, though the exact roles of the four layers shift slightly when you add materials and chemical sensors like gas or fire.
Here is how each piece handles your apartment scenario:

## 1. OpenUSD (The Apartment Container)

- **What it does:** You convert your apartment CAD drawing (`.dwg`, `.step`, `.rvt`) into an OpenUSD stage (`.usd`).
- **Your scenario:** OpenUSD acts as the master blueprint holding the 3D geometry of your walls, doors, sofa, chair, and gas cylinder in one unified coordinate space.

## 2. SimReady (Validation & Real-World Metadata)

- **What it does:** SimReady checks that your assets have clean geometry, real-world scale, and semantic labels.
- **Your scenario:** SimReady is actually where you tag what objects are and what they are made of (e.g., labeling the sofa as wood, the mirror as glass, and the cylinder as steel). It ensures your apartment assets aren't just hollow 3D shells, but carry valid physical attributes.

## 3. ovphysx (Physical Properties & Object Behavior)

- **What it does:** ovphysx uses those material assignments to calculate mass, density, friction, and collisions—not visual appearance.
- **Your scenario:**
  - Because your gas cylinder is tagged as steel, ovphysx calculates that it is heavy and won't tip over easily.
  - Because your shoes have rubber soles, ovphysx gives them high friction so they don't slide across the floor.
  - Because your chair is plastic, it is lightweight and reacts differently to impacts than the heavy wooden sofa.

## 4. ovrtx (Lights, Sunlight, Mirrors, & CCTV)

- **What it does:** ovrtx handles ray-traced light transport and optical sensors. It reads the visual shaders (MDL materials) linked to your SimReady objects.
- **Your scenario:**
  - **Sunlight & Lights:** ovrtx simulates exact sun angles through your windows based on time of day and geographic location, plus indoor LED bulbs.
  - **Glass Mirror:** Because the mirror is tagged as reflective glass, ovrtx accurately bounces light rays off it.
  - **CCTV Cameras:** ovrtx renders synthetic RGB, depth, or night-vision (IR) video feeds from virtual CCTV cameras mounted on your apartment walls.

## The One Catch: Gas and Fire Sensors

Neither ovrtx (light/optics) nor ovphysx (rigid-body mechanics) natively simulates chemical gas dispersion, smoke fluid dynamics, or thermal fire spread out of the box.

To simulate gas leaks or fire sensors in your apartment, you need an extra layer:

- **Fluid & Smoke Simulation:** You would use NVIDIA Flow (an Omniverse extension for real-time voxel-based fire and smoke) or import Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) data into OpenUSD.
- **Virtual Sensor Trigger:** Once smoke or gas volume reaches your ceiling sensor's coordinates in the USD stage, a Python script triggers a virtual alarm event.
