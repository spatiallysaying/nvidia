# Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab Workflow

Yes, your understanding is completely accurate.
In the NVIDIA robotics ecosystem, Isaac Sim provides the 3D virtual world and physics/sensor simulation (built on Omniverse technologies), while Isaac Lab sits on top of Isaac Sim as the specialized framework for training your robot's reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning policies.

## The Full Robotics Stack (Bottom to Top)

- **Foundation Layer (OpenUSD, SimReady, ovrtx, ovphysx):** This is the core engine layer. It loads your apartment CAD file, assigns physical materials (wood, steel, rubber), calculates rigid-body physics, and renders ray-traced lighting and CCTV cameras.
- **Simulation Application Layer (NVIDIA Isaac Sim):** Built directly on top of Omniverse, Isaac Sim acts as the high-level robotics simulator. It adds robotics-specific tools out of the box, such as URDF/MJCF robot importers, ROS 2 bridges, synthetic data generators, and articulated joint controllers for robotic arms or mobile bases.
- **AI & Policy Training Layer (NVIDIA Isaac Lab):** Built on top of Isaac Sim, Isaac Lab is an open-source, lightweight framework designed specifically for robot learning. Instead of simulating just one apartment at real-time speed, Isaac Lab spawns thousands of parallel copies of your apartment and robot on the GPU simultaneously, allowing your RL agent to practice walking, navigating around the wooden sofa, or avoiding the steel gas cylinder millions of times in a few hours.

## How Your Apartment Workflow Fits Together

1. **Build the World:** You prepare your SimReady OpenUSD apartment scene with all its physical materials.
2. **Import into Isaac Sim:** You load your apartment and your virtual robot (e.g., a quadruped, humanoid, or wheeled robot) into Isaac Sim to verify joints, sensors, and physics behavior.
3. **Train in Isaac Lab:** You wrap that scene in an Isaac Lab environment, define a reward function (e.g., +1 for reaching the kitchen, -10 for bumping into the glass mirror or steel cylinder), and train your policy using PyTorch and RL libraries like RSL-RL or SKRL.
4. **Deploy to Reality:** Once the policy succeeds in simulation, you export the trained neural network weights to your physical robot's onboard computer (such as NVIDIA Jetson).
